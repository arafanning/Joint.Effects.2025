# Joint effects of HIV and cannabis on neurocognition

**Published research · Dissertation study 1**

Adams, A. R., Lehman, S. M., Thompson, E. L., Kaiver, C. M., Lopez, D., Hawes, S. W., Lerner, B., Lopez-Quintero, C., & Gonzalez, R. (2025). Joint effects of human immunodeficiency virus (HIV) and cannabis on neurocognition. *Neuropsychology, 39*(6), 473–489. https://doi.org/10.1037/neu0001003

[Read the published article](https://doi.org/10.1037/neu0001003) · [Publicly available article text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12483287/)

## Research context

This study examined neurocognitive performance in a community sample grouped by HIV and cannabis-use status. Rather than assume that average scores adequately describe potential differences between groups, it also examined the likelihood of impairment within individual cognitive domains and variation associated with recent amounts of cannabis use.

The central distinction was between broad group comparisons and more specific patterns. Mean neurocognitive performance did not differ significantly across the four groups, but domain-specific impairment and associations with recent cannabis use were more informative under particular conditions. These cross-sectional associations do not establish causality.

This paper is the first of three studies comprising my dissertation research on cannabis, neurocognition, and everyday functioning in the context of HIV.

## Repository contents and limitations

Files named `01_setup.R` through `06_tables_figures.R` provide an **incomplete analysis scaffold**. Several scripts contain placeholders rather than the final executable analysis used for publication. This repository should **not** be treated as a complete, independently reproducible code release.

Participant-level data are not publicly distributed. Do not attempt to infer participant identities or reconstruct restricted study data from this repository. The published article is the authoritative source for the study methods and findings.
